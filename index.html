How to Calculate GPA from Marks and Credit Hours

FindMyGPA is a free online GPA calculator for university students. Enter your obtained marks, total marks, and course credit hours to estimate subject grades, individual GPAs, semester GPA, and overall marks percentage. The calculator uses an example 4.0 grading scale, so results may differ from your university's official grading policy.

GPA Calculation Formula

Overall GPA = Sum of (Subject GPA × Course Credit Hours) ÷ Sum of Counted Course Credit Hours

For example, if a subject has a GPA of 3.7 and carries 3 credit hours, it contributes 11.1 weighted grade points to the overall calculation.

How to Calculate Semester GPA in University

To calculate semester GPA, enter the marks obtained, total marks, and credit hours for every course taken during the semester. Multiply each subject GPA by its credit hours, add the weighted grade points, and divide the sum by the total counted credit hours. The result estimates your semester GPA using the grading scale applied by this calculator.

How to Calculate GPA from Percentage

First, calculate your percentage by dividing marks obtained by total marks and multiplying by 100. Converting that percentage into GPA requires a grading scale. Different universities may assign different GPA values to the same percentage, so use your institution's official conversion rules whenever available.

What Is the Difference Between GPA and CGPA?

GPA usually represents academic performance during a particular semester. Cumulative GPA, commonly called CGPA, represents academic performance across multiple semesters. A credit-hour- weighted CGPA can be calculated by adding weighted grade points across all included courses and dividing by their total counted credit hours, subject to your university's rules.

Note: The current FindMyGPA calculator calculates a weighted GPA from the subjects entered. It does not combine results from multiple semesters to calculate cumulative GPA.

How Do Credit Hours Affect GPA?

Credit hours determine how much each course contributes to a weighted GPA. For example, a 4-credit-hour course carries more weight than a 2-credit-hour course when their GPA values differ. Always follow your university's rules for repeated courses, failed courses, and other special grading situations.
